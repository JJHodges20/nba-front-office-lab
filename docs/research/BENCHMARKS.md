# What Contenders Have Looked Like

Benchmark research from the lab's own history of team seasons. Generated by `fol benchmarks --write` from build 4 of 2026-10-02; do not edit it by hand - change the data or the code and run the command again.

> Everything here describes seasons that are over: how often past teams of a kind reached each stage of the postseason, and what the teams that got there looked like. It is not a forecast for any team. There is one champion a season, so every share comes with the count behind it and an interval that says how far a share drawn from so few cases can be trusted.

- **All seasons.** Every season on record with a completed postseason.
- **Last 10 seasons.** The ten most recent completed seasons.
- **Last 5 seasons.** The five most recent completed seasons. Five champions and ten finalists: too few to say much about the later rounds.
- **Modern era.** A convention, not a fact: the seasons since pace and three-point volume began their climb to today's levels. League three-point attempts passed 28% of all shots in 2015-16.
- **Play-In era.** Since the play-in tournament for the seventh to tenth seeds began. Sixteen teams still make the playoffs, but twenty now reach the postseason.

Only seasons whose postseason is complete are counted. The eras are defined in `configs/eras.yaml` and can be changed there.

## How to read it

- **Stage.** Each stage contains every later one: *reached the postseason* - played in the playoffs or in the play-in tournament; *made the playoffs* - played at least one playoff game: one of the sixteen teams in the bracket; *won a playoff series* - won the first round and reached the conference semifinals; *reached the conference finals* - one of the last four teams; *reached the nba finals* - one of the last two teams; *won the championship* - won the nba finals.
- **Wins per 82.** Winning percentage times 82, so that seasons cut short by a lockout or a pandemic compare with the rest.
- **Band.** Teams are grouped in bands of a measure. A band holds its lower edge and stops short of its upper one: *45 to 50* holds 45 and not 50. A band is a way of grouping, not a threshold.
- **Interval.** The 95% Wilson interval for a share: the range the long-run rate plausibly lies in, given how few cases there are. *0 of 40* does not mean never.
- **League rank.** A team's rank on a metric among the teams of its own season; 1 is best. Ranks compare across eras where raw values do not.
- **Concordance.** Take a team that reached a stage and a team of the same season that did not. Concordance is how often the first has the better value of a metric: 50% is a coin flip, 100% is always. Its interval comes from drawing whole seasons again, because the teams of one season are not independent of each other.

## What separates them

All seasons, 1996-97 to 2025-26. One metric at a time: how often the team that reached a stage had the better value than a team of the same season that did not. Record and net rating sum up everything a team does and lead every table; the question is which ingredients come closest to them, and which do not separate teams at all. Rows run from the highest figure to the lowest. Near 50% a metric separates nothing; below it, the teams that reached the stage tended to have the worse value - or, for pace and three-point rate, the lower one.

## What this cannot say

- **It is description, not prediction.** A rate here is how often something happened to past teams in a band. It is not the chance that a particular team does it this season; that needs a model evaluated on seasons it has not seen.
- **One metric at a time.** The separation tables do not say what a metric adds once the others are known, and they say nothing about cause.
- **Regular-season figures only.** How a team played in the playoffs themselves is not used to describe it.
- **Nothing on roster make-up or payroll.** The lab holds player detail for recent seasons only and no contract history, so how contenders were built and what they cost cannot be measured yet.
- **Few champions.** One a season. Anything said about them rests on a few dozen teams at most, and on a handful in the short eras.
