# Do These Players Make Sense Together?

Roster fit: what a roster is described by, and whether any of it says something the sum of its players' impacts does not. Generated by `fol fit report --write` from revision 1 of the fit evidence (gathered 2026-10-04) and build 13; do not edit it by hand - gather the evidence again, rebuild, and run the command again.

> A roster's profile is description: averages of what its players did, for the minutes they played, and counts of the roles they fill by rules written down in `configs/archetypes.yaml`. Whether a profile predicts anything beyond the players' impacts added up is the question tested here, on seasons the test had not seen.

## What a roster is described by

The roadmap asks six questions of a roster - offense, defense, the possession battle, structure, depth and usage - and this is what thirty seasons of box scores, listed positions, the value model and the role rules can say about each. Every average weighs a player by the minutes played for the roster; roles are counted in the rotation, the ten players with the most minutes, and on the bench, the sixth to tenth. A real team is placed among the teams of its own season, so eras compare.

**Offense**

- *Primary creators*: Players in the rotation who fill the primary creator role.
- *Creators*: Players in the rotation who fill the primary or the secondary creator role.
- *Floor spacers*: Players in the rotation who fill the floor spacer role.
- *Three-point attempts*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' three-point attempts per 100 possessions on the court.
- *Three-point accuracy, regressed*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' three-point percentage with league-average attempts mixed in, so that a few hot nights do not make a shooter.
- *Free-throw rate*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' free-throw attempts per field-goal attempt: the nearest a box score comes to pressure on the rim.
- *Assist percentage*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' share of teammates' baskets assisted while on the court.
- *Offense, sum of parts*: The players' offensive impacts added up for the minutes given: the points per 100 possessions a team would gain on offense if its players simply added up.

**Defense**

- *Perimeter defenders*: Players in the rotation who fill the perimeter defender role.
- *Switchable wings*: Players in the rotation who fill the switchable wing role.
- *Rim protectors*: Players in the rotation who fill the rim protector role.
- *Steals*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' steals per 100 possessions on the court.
- *Blocks*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' blocks per 100 possessions on the court.
- *Defense, sum of parts*: The players' defensive impacts added up for the minutes given: the points per 100 possessions a team would save if its players simply added up.

**Possession battle**

- *Rebound percentage*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' share of available rebounds taken while on the court.
- *Turnover percentage*: Minutes-weighted average of the players' turnovers per play used.

**Structure**

- *Height, minutes-weighted*: Average listed height in inches, each player weighted by minutes.
- *Minutes by guards*: Share of the minutes played by players listed at guard, alone or with another position.
- *Minutes by wings*: Share of the minutes played by players of wing size listed at forward or at guard-forward. Guards and big men of that size are not wings here.
- *Minutes by big men*: Share of the minutes played by players listed at center or standing 6'11" and taller.
- *Minutes by two-position players*: Share of the minutes played by players listed at two positions: the nearest the listings come to positional flexibility. Athleticism is not in the data at all.

**Depth**

- *Bench impact*: Minutes-weighted average impact of the sixth to tenth players by minutes.
- *Bench creators*: Players among the sixth to tenth by minutes who fill a creator role.
- *Bench big men*: Players among the sixth to tenth by minutes who are big men.
- *Bench defenders*: Players among the sixth to tenth by minutes who fill a defensive role: perimeter defender, switchable wing or rim protector.
- *Minutes without a profile*: Share of the minutes played by players with no statistics to build a profile from: in a real season, newcomers to the league.
- *Reliance on one player*: The top player's wins above replacement as a share of the rotation's, counting only players above replacement. The higher, the more rests on one player staying fit.

**Usage**

- *Usage on the floor*: Five times the minutes-weighted average usage rate: what the players' usage adds up to with five of them on the court. A real team's adds up to 100%. A roster assembled from several teams can ask for more plays than a game has, or fewer.
- *Usage of the three highest-usage players*: The three highest usage rates in the rotation added up: how much of the offense three players are used to running.

**Sum of parts**

- *Impact, sum of parts*: The players' impacts added up for the minutes given: the net rating, relative to the league, a team would have if its players simply added up.
- *Projected impact, sum of parts*: The same with each player's projection going into the season instead of what the season showed.
- *Minutes covered*: The players' minutes as a share of the minutes a team has to fill in the season.

## How it was tested

The value model's own test predicted every team-season from 2006-07 on from its players, each taken alone, and recorded what each prediction missed. Fit would show in those misses, if it shows anywhere. Each fit measure was computed from the players' profiles of the season before (1 season back), weighted by the minutes they then played for the team, so that nothing of the season predicted leaks in; then it was asked, one season at a time with only earlier seasons to learn from, whether it explained the misses. The sums of parts themselves are what fit is measured beyond, so 27 of the 32 measures were tested.

- 510 team-seasons, 2009-10 to 2025-26, against value model r1 and roles v1.
- The value model's predictions missed by 2.96 points of net rating (RMSE) on those team-seasons.
- Each correction is least squares with an intercept on standardised measures. Intervals are 95%, from resampling whole seasons 2,000 times: the teams of one season play each other and are not independent.

## What it cannot tell you

- **The measures are proxies.** A box score records no screens, no switches, no closeouts and nobody's athleticism; the roles are rules on what it does record.
- **Nothing here says how players would play together.** A profile is an average over what each player did on the team they were on. The test asks whether such averages predicted anything beyond the parts; that is as far as it goes.
- **The test is bounded by the sample.** The 27 measures were tested on the seasons the value model's test covers, with the previous season's profiles. An effect too small to show there is not ruled out; it is only not found.
- **Roles follow the rules and the value model.** Change either and the counts move; every profile says which versions it rests on.
