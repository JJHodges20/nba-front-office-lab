# What a Season Could Bring

The scenario simulator: seasons drawn for a roster from parts the lab has tested, the few parameters measured for it, the test of the wins it gives, and the season ahead as it draws it for every team. Generated by `fol simulate report --write` from revision 2 of the simulator's parameters (measured 2026-10-05) and build 14; do not edit it by hand - rebuild, measure again, and run the command again.

> Every figure here is an estimate with an interval. A scenario's assumptions - who is on the roster, who misses games, who improves - are the user's; what they come to is the model's, and nothing is a guarantee.

## What a season is drawn from

A scenario is a roster for the season ahead - a real team as it stands, or a roster built in the Team Builder - with the user's changes: players added and removed, the games each is expected to miss, how far each comes out from the value model's projection, the share of the minutes each plays. Each simulated season then:

- draws every player's impact around the value model's projection, as far off as projections of players that well known have come out;
- draws the games every player misses from what rotation players have missed, around what the scenario assumes;
- adds the parts up for the minutes drawn - five slots of replacement level plus what each player adds above it - exactly as the optimizer and the Roster Fit page do, and draws the miss on a team's strength that the value model's test leaves once the players' own spread is taken out;
- adds a season's own noise to get the rating the season shows, and turns that into wins with the wins a point of net rating has been worth, plus the residual wins carry;
- reads the postseason model's season steps for the chance of each stage, with the interval its resampled steps give.

The value model (`r2`) and the postseason model (`p2`) were each tested on seasons they had not seen; their research documents say how. The parameters measured here are in `docs/decisions/0015-simulator.md`.

## The wins test

For each of the 600 team-seasons the value model predicted out of sample (2006-07 to 2025-26), 2,000 seasons were drawn from the predicted rating with the value model's miss and the residual wins carry, and the wins that happened were set against what the seasons gave.

| Interval | Held the wins that happened | Cases |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50% | 51.3% | 600 |
| 80% | 80.8% | 600 |
| 95% | 95.7% | 600 |

The median of the seasons drawn missed the wins that happened by 7.3 wins on average and by 9.1 root mean square, against 12.5 root mean square for giving every team an even record. An interval that holds the wins that happened about as often as it claims is one a reader can lean on; one that holds them more often is too wide, less often too narrow.

## What this does not say

- The field is not in it. Each team is drawn alone, as a team of its strength entering any season; the chances of the thirty teams need not add up, and a trade that strengthens one team does not weaken the other on this page.
- A roster is the parts added up. The value model found no fit measure that predicts beyond that, so none is used; how the players would play together is not modelled.
- Who misses games is drawn from what rotation players have missed, not from anyone's medical history, and development from how far projections have missed, not from any one player's trajectory. Both presets are assumptions, and a figure set for a player is the user's.
- The season ahead's projections rest on the value model's own limits: defense is the weak side, and projected minutes know nothing of depth charts or contracts.
- A chance is an estimate with an interval; the wins test is the evidence for reading it as one.
